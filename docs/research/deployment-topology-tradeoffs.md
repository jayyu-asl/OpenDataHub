# Host/YARN versus Kubernetes-led deployment trade-offs

**Research date:** 2026-11-02 (environment clock). **Decision consumer:** [Define deployment topology and resource-scheduling boundaries](https://github.com/jayyu-asl/OpenDataHub/issues/7); evidence ticket [Research host/YARN versus Kubernetes-led deployment trade-offs](https://github.com/jayyu-asl/OpenDataHub/issues/16), under [OpenDataHub — Product Definition, Architecture, and Feature Roadmap](https://github.com/jayyu-asl/OpenDataHub/issues/1).

## Decision frame and result

The human must select **one principal product route**, not two equally supported alternatives. No route is selected here. Rank **full lifecycle cost first**: disconnected deployment, upgrades, diagnosis, recovery and ongoing component/runtime adaptation. Performance is a workload-scoped acceptance gate, especially **interference among batch, long-running streams and interactive queries**, stability and overall utilization—not a universal peak-speed contest.

The fixed resolutions of [Define product personas, boundaries, and reference acceptance scenarios](https://github.com/jayyu-asl/OpenDataHub/issues/2#issuecomment-6567485286), [Define lakehouse data architecture and interoperability contracts](https://github.com/jayyu-asl/OpenDataHub/issues/8#issuecomment-6568627452) and [Define distribution support contracts and certified component combinations](https://github.com/jayyu-asl/OpenDataHub/issues/6#issuecomment-6569630098) remain binding: customer-operated, disconnected, multi-environment management; controlled Service Adoption; legacy Hive/Tez alongside shared Iceberg/HMS; separately selectable/certified HDFS and Ozone; dependency-complete Certified Paths, not arbitrary combinations. No catalog component is removed, shipping BOM chosen, or support policy reopened. Management-plane hosting is independent of the route comparison.

**Route-changing findings:**

- **CDP's host supervision claim is verified for Private Cloud Base 7.1.9:** Manager → per-host Agent → `supervisord` controls service roles. YARN schedules applications, not every platform service. This does not describe every CDP product. [V1], [V2], [V3]
- **TDH's Kubernetes substrate and retained YARN are documented, but “native Kubernetes replaces all process control/YARN” is too strong.** TDH 9.3.3 lists TOS/Kubernetes 1.16.1 and YARN/Hadoop 3.1.4; service management exposes Deployments/Pods, while YARN still has ResourceManager/NodeManager roles. Public material inspected does not resolve its AM/task-to-Pod mapping, nested resource accounting or all in-container supervision. [V4], [V5], [V6], [V7]
- **A complete Kubernetes-led route still owes the legacy execution stack.** External host YARN is a concrete compositional candidate, but retains host operations and a second scheduling domain. Containerized YARN services are not equivalent to native Kubernetes Tez tasks; neither HS2 Pods nor Spark-on-Kubernetes prove the selected Hive/Tez path. [T1], [T2], [T3], [V4], [V5], [V6], [V7]
- **Ozone table storage does not close auxiliary filesystem dependencies.** Tez 0.10.4 recovery calls `hflush()`. Ozone 2.0.0 and 2.2.2 gate hsync/hflush behind disabled-by-default, explicitly experimental enhancement settings; EC streams do not advertise those capabilities. An Ozone-selected path needs either an explicitly declared auxiliary HDFS dependency or a separately validated non-HDFS staging/recovery alternative. Neither is certified here. [F1], [F2], [F3], [F4], [F5]
- **No same-condition benchmark or lifecycle-cost measurement was established.** Host/YARN has a more direct documented fit for the fixed legacy path; Kubernetes offers a common desired-state surface for containerized services. Neither fact proves lowest total ownership cost for the complete catalog or best mixed-workload performance. The matrix below is bounded engineering inference, not a recommendation already accepted by the human.

## 2. Compare complete candidates, not scheduler labels

These are comparison envelopes, **not three promised support options**:

| Candidate | Service deployment and actual legacy execution | Boundary to cost and certify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H — host/VM services + YARN** | OS-managed/agent-controlled storage, catalog and service processes; RM/NMs launch Tez AM/tasks. Other engines/services use their declared host/process or YARN execution paths—not an assertion that everything runs in YARN. | OS/runtime/package and service adapters, host resources outside YARN, application HA, worker libraries/credentials and auxiliary filesystem closure. [V1], [T1], [T2] |
| **K-external — Kubernetes-led services + external host YARN** | Containerized eligible services/HS2 connect to host RM/NMs; **Tez AM/tasks still execute on YARN hosts**, accessing HMS, staging and selected data storage. Stateful services' host-versus-Pod placement must be explicit. | Two lifecycle/control domains and network/security crossings. Separate worker pools can isolate operations but strand capacity; shared hosts need non-overlapping resource budgets so both schedulers cannot count the same CPU/RAM. **I**, not evidence this exact product composition has run. [T1], [T2], [T3], [R1] |
| **K-contained — Kubernetes-led services including YARN compatibility services** | RM/NM services would be container-managed; actual Tez still needs a demonstrated YARN application launch path, shuffle, localization, credentials, staging and recovery. TDH documents the service substrate/roles, not enough public detail to reproduce this complete path. [V4], [V5], [V6], [V7] | Specify whether NM starts child processes inside its Pod, talks to a host runtime, or uses a documented bridge that creates task Pods. Account for nested cgroups, privileges, host mounts, NM identity and drain/recovery. Do not silently assume any one of these mechanisms exists. |

**Important distinction (D):** Hadoop 3.4.2 documents **NodeManager launching applications in Docker** via LinuxContainerExecutor, with Docker on NM hosts and security requirements. That is genuine documented containerized **application execution under YARN**, not proof of YARN services running in Kubernetes or Tez tasks scheduled by Kubernetes. Its guide recommends preloading large images to avoid startup timeouts. [T3]

Management API/UI/database may be hosted separately from any of these envelopes. A Kubernetes-hosted manager can manage hosts; a host-hosted manager can control Kubernetes. Neither selects service placement, storage placement or engine scheduling. Operation targeting must still isolate the customer's environments under the fixed product contract.

### Required execution and maintenance closure

- **D:** Hive's installation guide configures running YARN, Tez libraries and worker classpaths. Hive **4.0.1** pins Hadoop **3.3.6** and Tez **0.10.4**; do not combine this anchor with TDH's or Hadoop 3.4.2's versions and call it supported. Tez 0.10.4 localizes `tez.lib.uris`, uses a configurable staging directory, and stores DAG recovery state there. [T1], [T4], [F1]
- **I:** every candidate must identify HS2/Hive planning, HMS/database, actual RM/NMs/AM/tasks, shuffle auxiliary services, native/JVM/client libraries, local spill/cache directories, storage access, token/keytab renewal and history/log retention. Distributing only the HS2 image omits execution dependencies; independently deployed Spark does not fill them.
- **D:** Hive 4.0.1's compactor factory selects different paths by table type/configuration. Full ACID can use query-based Tez compaction or MR fallback; Iceberg major compaction invokes a query-based implementation that runs full-table `INSERT OVERWRITE ... SELECT`. Workers consume compaction requests. [T5] **I:** certify the chosen branches, worker runtime/identity and final compaction status. The fixed Iceberg rewrite reference is **not** native Hive ACID base/delta compaction; selected ACID compatibility needs its own maintenance evidence. Do not accidentally require MR for every case, or omit it if the selected configuration actually falls back to it.

### Ozone selection versus auxiliary HDFS

**D:** Ozone's Hive integration documents managed/external table data and client JARs; OFS can be `fs.defaultFS`, with special `/tmp` mounting semantics. This is a candidate filesystem interface, not proof of the whole Tez lifecycle. Hive scratch/job plans, `tez.lib.uris`, `tez.staging-dir`, DAG recovery, YARN aggregated logs and any selected MR staging/history paths must be inventoried separately from table locations. [F1], [F2], [T4]

There is a concrete semantic obstacle to check, not just an absent tutorial:

1. Tez **0.10.4** `RecoveryService` calls `hflush()` on summary and recovery streams. Its staging implementation uses Hadoop `FileSystem`, so HDFS is not hard-coded as the only possible backend. [F1], [F3]
2. Ozone **2.0.0** and **2.2.2** configurations default `ozone.fs.hsync.enabled=false`, gated by `ozone.hbase.enhancements.allowed=false`; the latter explicitly labels these enhancements experimental. Their Hadoop-3 stream capability implementations exclude EC streams and condition HFLUSH/HSYNC on enablement. Ozone 2.2.2's `KeyOutputStream.hsync()` also requires RATIS with replication factor greater than one. [F4], [F5] The Hive guide's support for EC **table data** therefore cannot certify EC **Tez recovery streams**. [F2]

**Decision implication (I):** “Ozone selected for analytical data” and “no HDFS daemon anywhere” are different claims. A small auxiliary HDFS is a **candidate Supported Dependency**, not a mandated second analytical backend; its HA, disks, security, upgrades and recovery still count toward lifecycle cost. Alternatively, certify explicit non-HDFS staging/localization/recovery paths with the actual client/server versions, durability, permissions, rename/flush and AM-restart behavior. This study establishes neither that HDFS is universally unavoidable nor that Ozone-only execution is production-ready. Never hide HDFS under an “Ozone-only” label, silently require dual analytical-storage installation, or substitute S3A without proving required semantics.

## 3. Decision matrix — lifecycle cost before performance

All comparative judgments are **I**, derived from the cited **D** mechanisms. No numerical cost score, staffing estimate or universal winner is justified.

| Dimension | H — host/YARN | Kubernetes-led — distinguish K-external / K-contained | Evidence and decision-sensitive condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Offline bootstrap and replacement** | OS repositories, runtimes, packages, agents, service artifacts and Hadoop libraries; fewer substrate layers for legacy execution, but still full-catalog closure. | Also bootstrap/recover Kubernetes, registry access, runtime, CNI/CSI, DNS, operator/operand/init images and CRDs. K-external keeps host closure too. Existing customer K8s reduces work only after admission—not by assuming a managed cluster. | CDP local package/parcel repos, TDH ISO/RPM plus product images, and Kubespray's explicit offline artifact classes show that **neither images nor packages alone suffice**. Avoid circular registry/cluster bootstrap. [L1], [V6], [V7] |
| **Stateful disks and placement** | Stable host directories, device identity, mounts, rack placement, decommission/rebuild and disk replacement remain service-specific. | StatefulSets supply stable identity/storage attachment, not application replication/recovery. Local PVs remain node-bound; remote/replicated volumes change latency/failure domains. Layering replicated volumes below already replicated HDFS/Ozone/Kafka can add write/network cost; direct local disks avoid that layer but require deliberate replacement procedures. | Document replicas across real host/rack failure domains, metadata disks versus data/spill/image disks, reclaim policy and fencing. TDH itself retains explicit disk/role operations despite containerization. [L2], [L3], [V6] |
| **Networking and host access** | Direct host endpoints and Hadoop locality are relatively direct; port/DNS/time/TLS/security configuration still owned. | Service/Pod addresses, CNI/MTU and possibly overlays/NAT add configuration. K-external requires routability from Pods to RM/NMs, storage and back to advertised endpoints. K-contained adds NM host-access/runtime decisions. | HDFS short-circuit reads require co-location, native libraries, domain socket and shared memory access—not merely Pods on the same node. Select network/storage modes before comparison. [L4], [T3] |
| **Process lifecycle and drift** | Agent/service adapters must implement desired state, configuration distribution, restart order, health and drift handling. Supervisor supplies process control, not data correctness. | Kubernetes reconciles declared resources; application operators can encode domain knowledge. Container immutability helps dependency repeatability but does not remove per-service configuration/migration semantics. K-contained adds YARN's child lifecycle. | Assign exactly one owner per configuration/resource and define emergency-edit reconciliation. TDH's stale-config/restart workflow illustrates application work beyond Pods. [V1], [V2], [V6], [L5] |
| **Upgrade and layered recovery** | Coordinate OS/JDK/native/Hadoop/client changes, application schemas and host rolling operations. Host agent failure and daemon failure differ. | Additionally coordinate Kubernetes API/skew, runtime/CNI/CSI, CRDs/operators and operands. K-external adds cross-domain order; K-contained adds compatibility-stack drain/restart. | Kubernetes minor upgrades cannot be skipped in the documented kubeadm path. etcd restoration is a control-plane operation, not restoration of HMS transaction state, storage metadata, Kafka offsets or Flink checkpoints. Binary/image rollback is not data rollback. [V2], [L5], [L6], [L7] |
| **Ongoing adaptation and diagnosis** | Per-service host/package/runtime integrations; diagnose process/configuration, scheduler and data layers. Existing Hadoop lifecycle tooling can reduce only the coverage it actually supplies. | Common Pod/event/resource APIs can improve uniform diagnostics; actual operator coverage and upgrade/recovery behavior determine savings. More layers add failure modes: admission, image pull, CNI, CSI, probes, cgroup/OOM, YARN and engine. | Count maintained adapters, runtime/security patches, failure reproduction and offline support-bundle collection—not installer lines alone. Neither vendor's documented manager is “just” its process runtime. [V1], [V6], [V7], [L5] |
| **Mixed-workload resource control** | YARN queues/capacity/preemption and configured cgroups control submitted workloads. Host services outside YARN need explicit reserved budgets/isolation; otherwise interactive services compete with batch/streams. | Pod requests drive placement; CPU limits throttle and memory limits can trigger OOM kills. Uniform accounting is possible for Pod workloads, not automatic for host YARN or escaped/nested tasks. K-external pool partitioning trades isolation against sharing. | A single scheduler name is not disk/network/cache isolation. Control batch admission, stream reservations/checkpoint headroom and query concurrency; measure interference, not just aggregate CPU. [R1], [R2], [R3] |
| **Utilization and startup latency** | Queue sharing can reclaim idle application capacity; Tez container reuse amortizes launch/localization but retained sessions can consume capacity. | Packing containerized services can share spare resources; image pulls/init/admission and worker startup add cold-path latency. K-contained may queue once for a Pod and again for a YARN allocation; this depends on its actual design. | Compare warm/cold caches, long-lived service/stream behavior and elastic admission under identical reservations. No “every query starts a Pod” or “YARN always starts fresh JVMs” assumption. [T3], [R1], [R2], [R3], [R4] |
| **Performance-sensitive I/O** | Data locality, storage replication, shuffle/spill, encryption and network/storage contention dominate many paths. Host deployment alone does not ensure locality or bandwidth. | Local PV/direct network and remote CSI/overlay paths have different costs. Pod placement may lose HDFS locality/short-circuit access; it may also improve controlled resource placement. K-external traffic crosses the chosen network boundary. | Hold physical disks, replication/EC, failure domains, caches, data layout and security settings constant; document deviations. No inherent container tax or benefit is quantified here. [L2], [L3], [L4], [R1], [R2], [R3] |

**How to use the matrix:** H reduces the unproven integration surface of the required legacy stack, not necessarily total catalog maintenance. K-external preserves that stack but cannot claim one unified lifecycle or freely shared resource pool. K-contained can reduce visible host-service variation only if its compatibility layer is demonstrably maintainable; otherwise it moves that work into images/operators/runtime privileges. Existing customer skills and admitted infrastructure change these costs. The human should select one route with explicit excluded/deferred variants and gate ownership—not interpret these columns as a dual-route support commitment.

## 4. Concrete certification gates before support claims

These are **future acceptance requirements, not tests run**. Each must bind a Certified Path/release, selected artifacts, responsible operator and quantitative envelope. They preserve the fixed support policy rather than inventing a BOM.

1. **Execution topology proof:** inventory management hosting, service placement and engine scheduling separately. Run representative legacy Hive SQL/Tez plus bounded Hive Iceberg rewrite/maintenance and selected ACID maintenance; capture RM application/AM/task IDs, actual worker placement, effective identities/JARs, compactor final state and reconciled rows/files/snapshots. For K-contained, publish the real NM→task launch mechanism, cgroup/resource boundary and privileges; HS2 health or Spark success is not a pass.
2. **Storage selection closure:** certify HDFS-selected and Ozone-selected paths separately. Enumerate canonical table, scratch, Tez library/staging/recovery, log/history, checkpoint and maintenance locations; no accidental `hdfs://` default in a claimed HDFS-free path. For a non-HDFS alternative, prove flush/durability, rename/permissions, resource localization/token renewal and AM/worker restart recovery. If auxiliary HDFS is required, disclose it and certify its lifecycle without advertising dual analytical-backend selection as mandatory.
3. **Disconnected lifecycle closure:** deny outbound access during clean bootstrap, application execution, restart, node replacement, upgrade and restore. Inventory OS/architecture-specific packages/runtimes/native libraries, images and all transitive tools/plugins/operators/connectors plus internal DNS/time/CA/identity services. Record undeclared downloads and bootstrap cycles as failures. Preserve the separate artifact/provenance/licensing admission gates; no legal conclusion is made here.
4. **Stateful placement/recovery:** document physical failure domains, local/remote volume semantics, replication/EC, metadata/data/spill separation, fencing and decommission. Demonstrate declared disk loss, node loss and metadata/control-plane restoration, then reconcile data/transactions/checkpoints. A replacement Pod or a volume snapshot alone is insufficient. Assign numeric recovery targets before publishing support.
5. **Lifecycle cost and adaptation evidence:** for the chosen catalog subset, inventory upstream lifecycle coverage versus first-party adapters; exercise one supported component/runtime patch and one substrate upgrade, including a failed intermediate step. Record operator actions/time, recovery branches, ongoing API/operator/client coupling and ownership of gaps. Compare dependency-complete routes, not mature vendor software against an empty custom implementation; do not invent staffing estimates.
6. **Mixed-workload interference gate:** use reproducible batch inputs, continuous duplicate-bearing Kafka/Flink events, Trino interactive queries and selected Hive maintenance. Establish isolated baselines, then concurrent steady load, bursts and a declared supported fault/recovery episode. Measure batch completion, stream backlog/query-visible freshness and checkpoint stability, interactive p95/p99 latency/timeouts, CPU throttling/OOM, disk latency/throughput, shuffle/network, scheduler wait, cold/warm startup, headroom and useful overall utilization. Reconcile results/replay correctness first. Set numeric limits and acceptable degradation for the path before certification; neither route wins by unboundedly starving another workload.
7. **Comparable conditions and diagnosis:** preserve hardware, physical storage/network, data/layout/scale, versions, security, replication, concurrency, reservations, caches and engine tuning; disclose unavoidable topology differences. Correlate operation→service/controller→scheduler→engine→storage evidence in an offline, redacted support bundle. Separate substrate failures from application/data recovery; performance claims require repeatable same-condition evidence, not vendor marketing.
8. **Operation isolation and adoption:** use two customer environments/clusters to prove targeting, credentials, configuration/drift ownership, capacity and maintenance isolation. Admit existing host services or customer K8s only through named compatibility/remediation/recovery checks; connection-only visibility is not Service Adoption. Reject failed/partial adoption explicitly.

## 5. Limitations and unresolved factual blockers

- **Vendor implementation detail:** TDH 9.3.3 public documents establish TOS/Pods and real YARN roles but not the complete AM/task launch, process-supervision or nested accounting contract, nor compatibility with the chosen upstream Hive/Tez target. Obtain version-specific first-party manifests/operational contracts or implementation evidence before treating this as a reproducible product foundation. The absence of that detail in the bounded material inspected is **not proof that TDH lacks the capability**.
- **Auxiliary filesystem:** no inspected source establishes a certified HDFS-free combination covering the required Tez recovery and all maintenance paths. Ozone 2.2.2 tagged source was checked specifically to avoid assuming the 2.0.0 limitation had disappeared; its rendered versioned Hive/hsync URLs returned 404, so source—not a guessed guide—is cited. Alternative versions/configurations need their own evidence.
- **Cost/performance:** no equivalent full-catalog host/Kubernetes benchmark, measured operational-effort comparison or current vendor support commitment for the listed historical substrate versions was established. The comparison cannot supply numeric TCO, a universal performance ranking or proof of arbitrary CDP migration/Service Adoption compatibility.
- **Access/scope:** `docs.transwarp.cn` timed out; the vendor's public `www.transwarp.cn/doc/...` pages were reachable and their embedded article content was read. Investigation was bounded to route-changing mechanisms, not exhaustive vendor reverse engineering. No private customer material, secondary-source technical assertions or legal conclusions are used.

These are gates for a bounded decision and subsequent release certification, not reasons to promise both routes indefinitely. **The principal-route choice remains with the live human decision in [Define deployment topology and resource-scheduling boundaries](https://github.com/jayyu-asl/OpenDataHub/issues/7).**
